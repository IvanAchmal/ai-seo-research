# AI-Powered SEO Content Production — Playbook / SOP

**Topic:** How to produce, distribute, and measure SEO content in an AI-first search landscape (2026)  
**Based on:** Research across 10 practitioners (see `sources.md`)  
**Last updated:** August 2026

---

## How to use this document

This is not a generic AI SEO checklist. It is an operational playbook built from conflicting expert advice, filtered through judgment calls, and designed to be executed in sequence. Every tactical recommendation below cites the source it came from.

**Recommended audience:** Marketing leads, SEO operators, and small teams (1–5 people) who need a repeatable system—not enterprise change-management programs.

---



## Phase 0: Align before you optimize (Week 1)

Before touching prompts, tools, or publishing workflows, establish why AI search matters to the business and how success will be measured.

- Define the business problem AI visibility solves—not the channel tactic list (source: Kevin Indig, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kevinindig_how-to-build-an-ai-seo-strategy-that-outlasts-activity-7455156200456790016-gyUh), 2026).
- Secure executive sponsorship and name a cross-functional owner for AI SEO; without this, tactic sprints stall (source: Kevin Indig, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kevinindig_how-to-build-an-ai-seo-strategy-that-outlasts-activity-7455156200456790016-gyUh), 2026).
- Replace traffic-only forecasts with scenario planning tied to business outcomes—e.g., “If we increase third-party brand mentions by 20%, assisted conversions may rise ~15%” (source: Kevin Indig, insights from [Growth Memo framework](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kevinindig/), 2026).
- Set a **12-month planning horizon**, not a 5-year content calendar; the product curve moves too fast for long-range SEO bets (source: Caleb Ulku, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/IxC9zwX-BiE?si=HzrprPAFqfUXYtv4), 2026).

**Exit criteria:** Written mandate, named owner, 3–5 commercial prompts that represent real buyer questions, and agreement on non-traffic KPIs (citations, share of voice, branded demand).

## Phase 1: Build measurement infrastructure (Week 1–2)

You cannot improve or defend AI SEO spend without a baseline.

### 1.1 Track four AI visibility metrics

Run commercial prompts (buyer-intent questions) across major AI platforms and record:


| Metric                   | What to measure                                                                        | Source                                                                          |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Market-weighted coverage | Is the brand mentioned? Weight Google + ChatGPT highest                                | Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026 |
| Position                 | Rank within the answer (target top 3)                                                  | Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026 |
| Share of voice           | Coverage + position vs. competitors                                                    | Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026 |
| Citation-source presence | Is your brand in the retrieval sources behind the answer? (sample 500–1,000 citations) | Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026 |


**Cadence:** Weekly or monthly review using a dedicated test email and manual prompt runs, or an analyzer tool (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).

### 1.2 Add traditional SEO and business metrics

- Track indexing rates and impressions in Google Search Console after each publish batch (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 2026).
- Measure branded demand and citation visibility—not just sessions—as AI Overviews decouple rankings from clicks (source: Neil Patel, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/dnjcBnQphKk?si=CHIinHe5-B_qu1BI), 2026).
- Treat visibility inside AI answers as a primary KPI when 75%+ of AI Mode sessions may end without a site visit (source: Kevin Indig, insights from [Growth Memo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kevinindig/), 2026).

## Phase 2: Build the knowledge base (Week 2–3)

The knowledge base is the “lead domino” for every downstream content, PR, and brand-communication workflow (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).

### 2.1 Required inputs

Collect and centralize:

- Brand messaging, offers, and positioning
- FAQs, objections, and customer language (from reviews, sales calls, support tickets)
- Case studies, proof points, and proprietary data
- Competitor entity coverage (what topics rivals already “own”)

Store in an AI project workspace (ChatGPT/Claude Projects) or a dedicated client knowledge-base tool (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).

### 2.2 Niche taxonomy (if scaling beyond a handful of pages)

If you plan programmatic or multi-niche production, invest heavily in structured niche context before generation:

- Audience profiles
- Pain points
- Monetization paths
- Content formats that perform
- Subtopics

This taxonomy injection is what separates useful pages from keyword-swapped filler (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).

**Rule of thumb from synthesis:** Spend ~60% of setup time on taxonomy/context if scaling programmatically (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).

## Phase 3: Control brand narrative (Week 3–4)

AI models largely repeat what they find online—they do not “decide” who you are (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).

### 3.1 Identify citation sources in your niche

1. Extract citations from AI answers for your commercial prompts (500–1,000 sample).
2. List the 5–10 platforms that appear repeatedly.
3. Prioritize those platforms—not a generic directory checklist (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).



### 3.2 Optimize third-party profiles

- Write one tight, keyword-aware brand description and reuse it across key profiles (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).
- Build review diversity on niche-relevant platforms (e.g., G2/Capterra for SaaS, Avvo for legal)—not just Google (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).
- Keep NAP, product descriptions, and USPs consistent everywhere (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).



### 3.3 Close gaps on your own site

Cover pricing, positioning, proof, and FAQs on your domain so AI systems do not rely on third parties to define your brand (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).

For local businesses: treat Google Business Profile as the primary entity surface—agents need structured, machine-readable data (hours, services, categories, reviews) (source: Caleb Ulku, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/IxC9zwX-BiE?si=HzrprPAFqfUXYtv4), 2026).

## Phase 4: Produce content (ongoing)



### 4.1 Choose your production model

**Model A — Editorial / case-study driven (default for most teams)**

1. Start from keywords validated via autocomplete + weak-SERP checks (source: Julian Goldie, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/2e7c2D221Ds?si=0PciDHQjnlcrpBzM), 2026).
2. Feed unique inputs into AI: case studies, workflows, opinions, first-hand data—not keyword prompts alone (source: Julian Goldie, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/2e7c2D221Ds?si=0PciDHQjnlcrpBzM), 2026).
3. Generate outline from knowledge base → AI-assisted draft → **mandatory human QA** (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).
4. Apply the publish filter: **If AI can easily create it without your input, do not publish it** (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).
5. Add what AI cannot fake: original data, screenshots, documentation, expert opinion (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).

**Model B — Programmatic / schema-driven (for qualified teams only)**

Use only when you have engineering capacity and a validated niche taxonomy:

1. Never ask AI for freeform page copy at scale—use strict JSON schemas (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).
2. Separate layers: AI fills JSON → validated files → React/components render presentation (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).
3. Use deterministic title templates, not AI-generated titles (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).
4. Prioritize resource pages (lists, checklists, guides) and interactive tools over comparison-only pages (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).
5. Apply the utility test: “Would this page still be useful if search engines didn’t exist?” (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).



### 4.2 Content structure for AI citation

Format pages for extraction:

- Clear H1–H3 hierarchy
- Short, scannable paragraphs
- Direct Q&A sections / FAQs
- Bullet lists and structured tables where appropriate (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026; Caleb Ulku, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/IxC9zwX-BiE?si=HzrprPAFqfUXYtv4), 2026).

For high-stakes topics, invest in bespoke assets—custom visuals, interactive tools, proprietary research—that AI cannot replicate (source: Ross Hudgens, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosshudgens_being-a-category-leader-can-sometimes-negatively-activity-7455605665018040320-L3vK), 2026).

### 4.3 Publishing cadence

- Ship in monitored batches; confirm indexing and impressions before scaling the next batch (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).
- Concentrate budget and effort on high-intent channels rather than spreading across low-signal surfaces (source: Neil Patel, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/dnjcBnQphKk?si=CHIinHe5-B_qu1BI), 2026).
- Reserve 10–15% of budget/time for experiments; core investment stays in proven demand (source: Neil Patel, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/dnjcBnQphKk?si=CHIinHe5-B_qu1BI), 2026).

## Phase 5: Earn off-site visibility (ongoing)

Brands are ~6.5× more likely to be cited via third-party sources than their own domains (source: Kevin Indig, insights from [Growth Memo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kevinindig/), 2026).

### 5.1 “Link building 2.0” — retrieval-source mentions

1. Identify sites already appearing in AI citations for your target keywords.
2. Earn repeated brand mentions on those sources—follow, nofollow, and even unlinked mentions may influence recommendations (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).
3. Aim to appear alongside established brands in the same source set (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).



### 5.2 Community presence (Reddit)

Reddit is a high-trust retrieval source for AI answers—but treat it as a **layer**, not your entire strategy (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).

**Reddit SOP (6 steps):**

1. Build a low-friction profile (no sales bio, normal avatar) (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).
2. Lurk 7–14 days per subreddit; learn rules, tone, and upvote patterns (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).
3. Use a two-tier subreddit strategy: niche communities first, larger subreddits after karma/history (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).
4. Comment early on Rising/Hot threads with scannable, expert value (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).
5. Use proven comment formats: straight answer, missing piece, story drop, proof point, mini playbook (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).
6. After credibility is earned, publish high-value threads (case studies, templates, lessons learned) with clear headings and bullets (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).

**Pro tip:** Find subreddits where Reddit threads already rank on page 1 for your target Google keywords—these are often the same sources AI systems read (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).

### 5.3 Distribution beyond your site

Invest in “internet depth”—newsletters, founder branding, PR, and multi-platform distribution (source: Ross Simmonds, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_marketing-breakingnews-google-activity-7456006446032273408-je0N), 2026).

Distribute to channels LLMs frequently cite: Reddit, LinkedIn, Quora, Medium (source: Ross Simmonds, insights from [Foundation Marketing philosophy](https://www.linkedin.com/in/rosssimmonds/), 2026).

Use AI coding tools to ship technical SEO fixes, launch tools, and update templates without engineering bottlenecks (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026; Caleb Ulku, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/IxC9zwX-BiE?si=HzrprPAFqfUXYtv4), 2026).

## Phase 7: Review and reallocate (monthly)

- Re-run commercial prompts; update coverage, position, share of voice, and citation-source metrics (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).
- Review GSC indexing and impression trends per batch (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).
- Reallocate budget mid-quarter toward channels with improving intent signals; cut channels that no longer provide clearer intent than last year (source: Neil Patel, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/dnjcBnQphKk?si=CHIinHe5-B_qu1BI), 2026).
- Feed performance data back into niche taxonomy and content-type priorities (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).

## Where experts disagree



### 1. How much to automate publishing

**Julian Goldie** runs Claude Code in low-permission mode to generate, deploy, and index articles across multiple sites in parallel—with browser automation for indexing and spreadsheet tracking—optimizing for speed and volume (source: Julian Goldie, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/2e7c2D221Ds?si=0PciDHQjnlcrpBzM), 2026).

**Nathan Gotch** insists on human-in-the-loop QA for every asset and applies a hard filter: if AI can easily create the content without your unique input, do not publish it (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).

**My call:** Side with Gotch for this playbook’s default workflow. Goldie’s approach is valid for rapid testing on owned properties, but the assignment criteria and long-term brand risk favor mandatory human QA before anything goes live. Automate drafting, formatting, and deployment—not quality judgment.

---



### 2. Scale vs. precision in content production

**Jake Ward** generated 13,000+ pages in under 3 hours using schema-driven programmatic SEO and reported +466% traffic in 60 days (source: Jake Ward, [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/programmatic-seo-2026-how-i-built-13000-pages-3-hours-jake-ward-pcjke/), 11.03.2026).

**Neil Patel** argues that spreading effort across channels is losing ground; teams should concentrate on fewer high-intent environments, and organic social budgets are collapsing because most brands cannot produce entertainment-grade content (source: Neil Patel, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/dnjcBnQphKk?si=CHIinHe5-B_qu1BI), 2026).

**Ross Simmonds** adds that publishing generic content at scale gets weaker as users gain more control over preferred sources (source: Ross Simmonds, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_marketing-breakingnews-google-activity-7456006446032273408-je0N), 2026).

**My call:** Ward’s model is real but conditional—it requires engineering infrastructure, niche taxonomy, and batch monitoring most teams lack. Default to Patel/Simmonds’ precision-first approach; graduate to Ward’s programmatic model only after Model A proves demand in a niche.

---



### 3. Paid placements for AI visibility

**Nathan Gotch** states sponsored posts and paid placements on retrieval-source sites can still influence AI answers, and that follow/nofollow/unlinked mentions all appear to matter (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026).

**Ross Hudgens** frames AI visibility as a reputation and authority problem—you cannot bulldoze into recommendations for topics your brand lacks recognized authority in (source: Ross Hudgens, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosshudgens_being-a-category-leader-can-sometimes-negatively-activity-7455605665018040320-L3vK), 2026).

**My call:** Treat paid mentions as a limited testing lever, not a core strategy. They may shift short-term citation patterns, but Hudgens is right that sustained AI recommendations require genuine topical authority. Use paid placements only on sources you would be proud to appear on organically.

---



### 4. Reddit as primary channel vs. supporting layer

**Matt Diggity** presents Reddit as the key to AI search, with case-study results of 2,814% AI referral traffic growth (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026).

**Kevin Indig** warns against building strategy on any single third-party platform cycle; off-site authority matters, but tactics without business alignment fail (source: Kevin Indig, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kevinindig_how-to-build-an-ai-seo-strategy-that-outlasts-activity-7455156200456790016-gyUh), 2026). Research notes also flag platform cycles: rank well → SEOs flood → quality drops → platform pivots.

**My call:** Include Reddit as Phase 5.2, not Phase 1. It is high-leverage for AI citation, but dependency on one platform is fragile. Own your domain narrative first; use Reddit to amplify retrieval-source presence.

## What I rejected and why



### 1. Multi-site parallel publishing without differentiation (Julian Goldie)

Goldie publishes the same workflow across five websites simultaneously, using parallel agents and automated indexing (source: Julian Goldie, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/2e7c2D221Ds?si=0PciDHQjnlcrpBzM), 2026).

**Rejected because:** This optimizes for speed of footprint, not durable brand authority. Cross-site duplication increases quality-risk, splits entity signals, and conflicts with Ross Hudgens’ warning that incoherent brand coverage hurts LLM consensus (source: Ross Hudgens, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosshudgens_being-a-category-leader-can-sometimes-negatively-activity-7455605665018040320-L3vK), 2026). One strong domain beats five thin ones for most teams.

---



### 2. Extension domains as a default scaling tactic (Koray Tuğberk GÜBÜR)

Koray demonstrates extension domains that expand topical maps with strong traffic results—e.g., clicks from 5.1K to 16.5K on an extension property (source: Koray Tuğberk GÜBÜR, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/koray-tugberk-gubur_this-is-an-example-of-an-extension-domain-activity-7453208301976174592-ttW5), 2026).

**Rejected as default because:** This is advanced semantic architecture that requires deep topical mapping expertise, design consistency, and inter-domain strategy. For most operators, it adds complexity and brand fragmentation risk before they have maximized their primary domain. Include only as an optional Phase 8+ expansion after topical authority is established on the main site.

---



### 3. “Panic-testing” AI tactics without organizational alignment (Kevin Indig)

Indig describes teams jumping into prompt optimization, entity markup, and citation acquisition without shared KPIs or executive mandate (source: Kevin Indig, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kevinindig_how-to-build-an-ai-seo-strategy-that-outlasts-activity-7455156200456790016-gyUh), 2026).

**Rejected as a starting approach because:** The playbook explicitly front-loads Phase 0 alignment. Tactics without measurement and sponsorship produce non-reproducible wins that cannot be scaled or defended.

## My original ideas



### Citation-source gap score (not found in any source)

**Concept:** For each commercial prompt, calculate:

`Gap Score = (% of top citation sources mentioning competitors) − (% mentioning your brand)`

Rank gaps by source authority and mention frequency. Prioritize off-site work on the top 3 gap sources per prompt cluster.

**Why it could work:** Nathan Gotch tells you to extract citations and get visible on trusted sources (source: Nathan Gotch, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/mbCfRlY7elM?si=i8L4Q-XorwT-lR2L), 2026). Kevin Indig emphasizes third-party citation advantage (source: Kevin Indig, insights from [Growth Memo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kevinindig/), 2026). Matt Diggity tells you which platforms AI reads (source: Matt Diggity, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/f03ZfdtkMzk?si=Q69h5vjczSPQkRGb), 2026). None combine these into a single prioritization formula. A gap score turns citation analysis from a report into a weekly action queue—fix the highest-gap source first, re-test prompts, repeat.

## Weaknesses of this playbook

1. **Survivorship bias in case studies.** Ward’s +466% and Diggity’s +2,814% results come from skilled operators with existing infrastructure. Replication by a beginner team is not guaranteed.
2. **Tool vendor overlap.** Nathan Gotch promotes Rankability; Julian Goldie promotes Claude Code and his agency/community; Jake Ward promotes Byword. Recommendations may be partially shaped by commercial incentives.
3. **Local vs. non-local gap.** Caleb Ulku’s GBP/entity guidance is strong for local SEO but this playbook targets general content production. Non-local B2B SaaS teams may need different entity strategies.
4. **Citation measurement is still manual.** AI platforms change outputs frequently; weekly prompt runs may not capture variance. No source provided a fully reliable, reproducible measurement standard.
5. **Indexing lag is underweighted.** Ward reported ~50% of 13,000 pages indexed after 60 days. Teams expecting fast ROI may abandon tactics prematurely.
6. **Regulatory and platform-risk blind spots.** Reddit bans, Google helpful-content updates, and Preferred Sources personalization (source: Ross Simmonds, [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_marketing-breakingnews-google-activity-7456006446032273408-je0N), 2026) can shift results faster than this playbook can be updated.
7. **Missing: influencer/creator distribution playbook.** Neil Patel shows 78% influencer budget growth (source: Neil Patel, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/dnjcBnQphKk?si=CHIinHe5-B_qu1BI), 2026), but this SOP does not yet operationalize creator partnerships—only owned and community content.

## Who I would NOT recommend following (and why)



### Julian Goldie — not as a primary strategic guide

Goldie demonstrates impressive automation with Claude Code—multi-site publishing, parallel agents, automated indexing (source: Julian Goldie, [YouTube](https://youtu.be/2e7c2D221Ds?si=0PciDHQjnlcrpBzM), 2026). He is useful for **tactical inspiration** on AI tooling.

However, I would not recommend him as a primary strategist because:

- The workflow optimizes for volume and speed over brand coherence and long-term authority.
- Quality control is mentioned but subordinated to automation (“leave it running in the background”).
- The approach closely promotes his paid community and agency funnel, which raises questions about generalizability.
- It conflicts with stronger consensus from Gotch, Hudgens, and Patel on quality, measurement, and concentration.

**Use Goldie for:** workflow ideas. **Do not use Goldie for:** your core content strategy or quality bar.
